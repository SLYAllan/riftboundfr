The first regional tournaments in Sydney and Suzhou in Unleashed solidified what the best-of-1 metagame pointed at: Aurora is going to be a very strong synergy for this metagame. In Xi'an, the other legends fought back, with gear removal included in the main deck to edge the first game, and more available in the side.

In this hostile environment, most legends built around the 9-cost gear posted their weakest performance across the three regional tournaments we've had in Unleashed so far. Notably, Sivir stopped in Top 32 after being a potential winner in Sydney and Suzhou while Poppy went from a dark horse nobody expected to a top 256 performer.

On the other hand, midrange or control oriented legends did pretty well in Xi'an. Azir won the tournament against Diana in the finals while Irelia, Vex and LeBlanc added another top 8 to their pedigree. We could also note the good results from Lux, Vi, Kha'Zix or Darius, all posting or matching their best run in a regional tournament so far.

The situation heading into Vancouver is quite simple to be fair, but a puzzle to solve:

- Either one expects Aurora to not be very popular due to the beating it took in Xi'an, and builds towards beating the likes of Irelia, Diana, Master Yi or LeBlanc.
- Or we probably copy, or largely get inspired by the lists from the latest regional, hoping for Aurora to remain a popular synergy.

To be fair, there isn't an obvious answer. Indeed, not packing the appropriate answer to Aurora means playing at a huge disadvantage every time we go against it. However, unless one is a fantastic pilot, you can't expect to go undefeated against the top 4 legends in Riftbound with a list tailored for another matchup.

If anyone finds the perfect balance to cover both ends of the spectrum, you'll probably see them in Vancouver's top 8. Otherwise, the upcoming regional tournament in Canada might be a gamble, at least when it comes to the flexible cards in our deck.

## Tier 2

With another Top 8 in Vancouver, Vex would build a very strong case to reach Tier 1, but the Gloomist's performance in the best-of-1 metagame fails to compare to the four above. Similarly, Sivir would also return to that first group if she shows her ability to adapt to a metagame now designed to beat Aurora in game one.

The other five legends in this group feel a step behind the top tier competitors, but most are one big performance away from becoming serious threats. Indeed, Azir has been a surprise winner in Xi'an, but now needs to prove it can perform when the field respects it as a top cut contender. Most likely, if Sivir finds a way to perform in Vancouver, Miss Fortune will follow along, as both legends basically do their homework together at this point.

Ezreal, Fiora and Kha'zix proved they are competitive legends with three solid performances in as many regional tournaments. However, except for Ezreal's top 8 in Suzhou to kick off regional qualifiers, these three failed to look like potential winners.

## Tier 3

To have a certain potential means you have earned some respect. However, it could also mean you fail to be consistent enough to be considered more than just potential. This is exactly how I feel about most legends in this tier. From Teemo to Lux, they all have some potential, as attested by their ability to routinely challenge for a Top 64, earning their pilot a continental invite.

However, it would also be a shock around the community if any of those managed to reach the top 8 of a regional competition. Indeed, when Teemo did it in Sydney, it was all the chat could talk about, while Poppy's top 4 in Suzhou was quickly dismissed as an "Aurora is busted" discussion with the legend barely being mentioned.

Overall, the battle for best-of cards around these legends should be fascinating to follow, both because they are good enough to be day 2 troublemakers, but also because there aren't enough of those legends in tournaments to be sure the best list has been found yet.
