Top 128 Suzhou / Top 256 Sydney 6.6 Renata Glasc 6.6.0.0.1 Performance - Top 256 Suzhou / Top 256 Sydney 6.7 Ivern 6.7.0.0.1 Performance - Top 256 Suzhou / Top 512 Sydney This was a tournament packed weekend for Riftbound, with both regions holding their first regional qualifier tournament. Combined, Sydney and Suzhou represent over 2,000 competitors, competing in those main events and smaller tournaments as well. At the end of it, the original Master Yi and Irelia solidified their position atop the metagame, both winning a regional qualifier each. In that battle to be the best legend in Riftbound, the Blade Dancer took the lead, largely thanks to the players using OGN-056 in their main deck as a way to overcome the otherwise daunting Aurora match-up. Speaking of OGN-160 , the card was the talk of the weekend, powering Sivir to the third spot in our rankings, Miss Fortune to win two tournaments, and Poppy to be a surprise semifinalist in Suzhou. Add to these three Fiora posting her best performance in Sydney using that gear instead of OGN-242 , and it amounts to four of the fourteen legends ranked in the first two tiers building around the 9-cost gear. There is no doubt the card is very strong, and will continue to be a big part of the Unleashed metagame if Riot doesn't step in. For sure, most players looking to earn a metal card with a legend within the body domain is considering OGN-160 at some point. However, the gear also failed to win anything big, while Irelia, a legend considered weak against Aurora, already found a way to navigate that match-up. In that context, it is difficult to say whether Riot should do something about that strategy, or let the community handle that problem for the time being. After all, we also had a few surprises from other legends this weekend, such as Teemo, Ezreal, or Vex ,whom many didn't consider top cut material. Who knows, maybe Aurora was only an answer for those without enough time to learn more intricate strategies. Tier 1 Irelia and Master Yi entered the first regional tournament as the top two legends, and lived up to that moniker. Indeed, even if the Wuju Bladesman couldn't go further than the top 16 in Sydney, he still won Suzhou and posted the best performance across all the smaller tournaments happening over the week-end. As for the Blade Dancer, she was one game away from winning both regional tournaments with very similar builds. Many players were worried for Irelia due to her tough match-up against OGN-160 . Yet, it seems like she found a solution for that match-up, with OGN-056 included in the main deck. Although she is a step behind these two, Sivir deserves to be in that Tier 1 conversation. Indeed, the Battle Mistress reached the finals in Sydney, and the semifinal in Suzhou. At the moment, Sivir is the best legend built around OGN-160 , who made a triumphant return thanks to UNL-118 . Irelia Performance - Top 2 Suzhou / Wins Sydney alongside 1 Win / 2 Top 8 in other tournaments Master Yi - Wuju Bladesman Performance - Wins Suzhou / Top 16 Sydney alongside 1 Win / 15 Top 8 in other tournaments Sivir Performance - Top 4 Suzhou / Top 2 Sydney alongside 3 Top 8 in other tournaments Tier 2 Both the Sydney and Suzhou tournaments revealed certain legends were much more competitive than the early events indicated. Indeed, heading into the first regional tournaments, most would have their money on Diana, LeBlanc and Fiora as most likely to challenge the top tier trio. It is only fair as these three were posting great results in Best-of-1 tournaments. Both new Unleashed releases solidified their position as great performers with a top cut in Sydney, but failed to look dominant in Suzhou. To be fair, LeBlanc almost missed out on the top cut if it wasn't for a different take brought by the best performer in Sydney. Diana played closer to the lists we already knew, but might lack a bit of raw power in order to be dominant. Miss Fortune, Sett and Annie were also expected to compete for top cut spots, even if they all came up short. The Bounty Hunter could just copy Sivir's best list, while the Boss and the Dark Child can count on a few players in the western scene to find a way to refine their built. Vex was regarded as a Dark Horse heading into the first regional tournaments. His champion unit, UNL-150 has become one of the most impactful cards in this new set, while both the Calm and Chaos domains are stacked with excellent disruptive cards. In that context, those able to build an early lead and force their opponent in a reactive position early on are thriving with Vex, while players with a more passive, reactive type of build didn't find as much success. Last, Poppy, Ezreal and Teemo are the big winners of the weekend, all three flying under the radar, but emerging as top cut contenders. Following its win in an online tournament held in the West, Ezreal hinted at its potential in the right hands. Ironically, its top cut performance happened in the Chinese regional tournament, beating even Diana in that one. Just like most Body legends, Poppy is using the sheer power of OGN-160 . The Chaos domain remains the best pairing, but there are several fantastic units and spells in the Order domain as well. Teemo surprised everyone with a build leaning heavily on an explosive start followed by hidden cards keeping the opponent at bay. This is the legend to keep an eye on going forward, to assess whether that was a lucky run for an otherwise weak legend, or if that list can keep Teemo in business for the Unleashed set. In that sense, we could call Tier 3 the inconsistent pack of Riftbound legends at the moment. If we take a closer look, we'll notice most of these legends rely on a specific synergy or play style they can't deviate from, no matter the metagame. As such, I would expect their strong performance to come from a player with a deep understanding of their legend, able to build a deck tailored for the environment they expect for that tournament. When you strike the right build, these look able to put up a fight in the final rounds, competing for a possible top cut spot. In that scenario, several legends in this tier can be considered on Sett, Azir or Annie's power level. However, both the pilot and the build have to be on point, or you might also be out early in Day 2, or stay out of it entirely. I wouldn't recommend picking from this group for anyone looking to earn a best-of card. You might not have as many players contesting you for that prize, but reaching the top 64, or even 128, requires a good understanding of both the legend, and the match-ups you expect to go against. Overall, these are solid picks for someone looking to play off-meta, but also with enough time to learn what play-patterns or match-ups their legend can rely upon in order to win 7 to 9 rounds in a regional qualifier tournament.
