The similarities are clear on a macro level. Irelia, Draven and Kai'Sa remain the top three legends in terms of popularity, while Viktor, Master Yi, Rek'sai, Fiora or Ezreal trailing them is pretty close to the pre-ban play rates as well. If we look at conversion rates into day 2, Viktor and Fiora struggle among popular legends, a typical occurrence for one known to struggle on the big stage, and a Grand Duelist who never really made in the western metagame. On the other side of the spectrum, Draven and Irelia took first and second in that category, just like we saw it happen in every tournament in the first part of Spiritforged. To notice big differences, we have to look at the tournament's top 8. First, we have 5 different legends ranging across 5 different domains, with only Mind being left out. In terms of diversity, only Bologna did it better, with 5 legends and all domains represented. Keep in mind that tournament was extremely close to the western release, and the place where Miracle builds emerged for the first time. Vegas the following week was a completely different story. Then, with all three Irelia players losing in the quarterfinals, four different legends battled it out for the title. Among these four, only Draven was on most people's radar as a possible winner. Indeed, Azir was still considered weaker than Viktor, with a significant part of the community calling its 2nd place in a Chinese regional tournament a fluke. Annie failed to post any significant results since winning that finals against Azir back in China. Last, Master Yi posted solid, but far from regional winner material type of results. I'm certain Atlanta won't be as diverse as Lille was, just like Vegas took notes from Bologna and focused on those strategies. However, I don't think this weekend's tournament showed something that needs to be either played or targeted. As such, we might close Spiritforged with a great event to watch. Expect Irelia and Draven to once again be the most popular picks, and probably again look dominant from afar. After all, both remain very strong proactive legends, meaning they are perfectly suited for events with over a thousand competitors. However, as the tournament progresses, and only the best players are left, expect to once again be surprised by the legends still alive. Indeed, just like it happened in Lille with Azir or Master YI, maybe a tiny lack of brute power might be a positive, as you won't have to worry about the field targeting you with their battlefields or side decks.
